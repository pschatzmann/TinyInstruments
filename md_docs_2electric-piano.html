Electric Piano

samples/Wurlitzer.h contains recordings of a Wurlitzer EP200 electric piano from A1 to A♭6: 20 notes with 4 velocity layers (pp, mp, f, ff). The loud layers have the characteristic "bark" of the Wurlitzer, which is typical for soul, blues and rock. It needs about 700 KB of flash.

Source: Greg Sullivan's E-Pianos, Wurlitzer EP200 v1.1 by Greg Sullivan, SFZ mapping by kinwie. License: CC-BY 3.0: unlike the other sample sets (CC0), the use requires attribution, e.g. "Wurlitzer samples by Greg Sullivan (CC-BY 3.0)".

Smaller versions: WurlitzerSmall() (~250 KB, samples/WurlitzerSmall.h) and WurlitzerOctave() (~150 KB, samples/WurlitzerOctave.h), see Versions and sizes. They keep the loudest layer (with the bark): soft notes are created from it with the velocity lowpass.

#include "samples/Wurlitzer.h"
Instrument epiano = ElectricPianoInstrument(Wurlitzer());
TremoloEffect tremolo(5.5f, 0.3f); // optional: the built-in "vibrato" of the Wurlitzer
...
synth.setInstrument(-1, epiano);
synth.setEffect(-1, &tremolo);

See the jazz-trio-serial-midi example.

Listen to an example (ii - V - I with jazz voicings, a melody, tremolo).

Playing

ElectricPianoInstrument():

  • 10 notes, all keys have dampers (short release), sustain pedal
  • the velocity selects the layer (pp ... ff) and the brightness
  • little stereo spread; volume 0.6 (the recordings are louder than the other instruments)

Tremolo

The TremoloEffect is the amplitude modulation of the Wurlitzer (called "vibrato" on the instrument). With stereo = true the left and right channel are modulated in opposite phase: the auto pan of a Rhodes suitcase piano. It can also be used for guitars or organs.

TremoloEffect tremolo(5.5f, 0.3f, false); // rate (Hz), depth (0..1), stereo
tremolo.setDepth(0.5f);
tremolo.setRate(4.0f);
