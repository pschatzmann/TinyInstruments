Upright Bass

samples/UprightBass.h contains pizzicato recordings of an acoustic double bass (1958 Otto Rubner, fifths tuning CGDA) from A0 to C4: 14 notes in minor thirds with 2 velocity layers. The walking bass of a jazz trio. It needs about 700 KB of flash.

Source: Karoryfer Meatbass (CC0).

Smaller versions: UprightBassSmall() (~250 KB, samples/UprightBassSmall.h) and UprightBassOctave() (~150 KB, samples/UprightBassOctave.h), see Versions and sizes.

#include "samples/UprightBass.h"
Instrument bass = UprightBassInstrument(UprightBass());
...
synth.setInstrument(1, bass); // e.g. midi channel 2

See the jazz-trio-serial-midi example (electric piano, upright bass and drums).

Listen to an example (walking bass).

Playing

UprightBassInstrument():

  • up to 4 notes at the same time (one per string), no amplifier
  • the velocity selects the layer and the brightness: soft notes are dark
  • vibrato with the modulation wheel (CC1), pitch bend
  • notes between the recorded notes are created with PSOLA (PitchShift::Psola), which keeps the attack and the length

Conversion

The original recordings (4 velocity layers, 4 round robins) were reduced to the softest and the loudest layer, first round robin (--max-layers 2). The loud C4 recording contains a low thump instead of the note, so the f layer is used for it. A plucked string is a little sharper at the start and drifts in pitch, so the tuning is corrected with the pitch after the attack (--tune-correct): all notes are within about 11 cents.
